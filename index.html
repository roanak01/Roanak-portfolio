01

ORIGINAL SHORT FILM · WRITTEN & DIRECTED

REPRISE

COMING SOON
◌
24 HOURS

What if knowing exactly when you will die is enough to make you see death everywhere?

REPRISE follows a young man who becomes convinced that he has only 24 hours left to live. As the hours pass, ordinary people, places and moments begin to feel like warnings — until fear starts collapsing the distance between what is real and what exists only inside his mind.

Written and directed as a self-produced short film, REPRISE is an attempt to explore paranoia, mortality and perception through a deliberately restrained visual language.

Made with limited resources, the film has gone from screenplay to production and is currently in its final stages of editing.

CINEMATIC VIDEO · AI & CREATIVITY

Is AI Killing Creativity?

↗

What happens when something we've always considered deeply human becomes easier to create with a machine?

An experimental cinematic video exploring the uncomfortable relationship between artificial intelligence and human creativity.

Rather than treating AI simply as a tool that replaces creativity, the film explores the uncertainty around what happens when ideas, images and stories that once required years of practice become increasingly accessible to everyone.

AI FILM CONCEPT · PRE-PRODUCTION

WHO AM I?

✦
Many forms. Many realities. One consciousness.

A being who exists across countless forms begins to question his own existence — only to discover that he may be nothing more than a generated construct in someone else's creation.

A philosophical AI film concept exploring identity, consciousness and creation.

The story follows a being who exists in different forms and realities — a child, an adult, an old man, an alien, a god — until he begins to question whether he is transforming at all, or simply being rewritten.

The project is currently in pre-production, with visual development and concept work underway.

03 / ABOUT

Still learning.
Still making.

I don't think I've figured out what kind of filmmaker I want to become yet.

I'm still learning — by writing, making mistakes, shooting things on whatever camera I have, editing them, watching films and trying again.

I'm especially drawn to psychological and philosophical stories, atmosphere, human behaviour and films that leave a question behind rather than simply answering one.

My interest in AI came from the same curiosity: understanding whether new technology can give a filmmaker more ways to think, visualise and experiment without taking away the human intention behind the story.
